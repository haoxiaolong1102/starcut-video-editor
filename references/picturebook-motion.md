# PICTUREBOOK MODE

Use when real material is unavailable, a story or emotion needs staging, an abstract scenario needs a concrete metaphor, or continuous presenter footage creates fatigue.

## Continuity bible before generation

Define once:

- character identity, face, age range, silhouette, clothing, props
- palette, line weight, texture, paper/collage language
- locations, time of day, light direction, camera direction
- fixed aspect ratio, negative space, brand constraints
- reusable foreground, midground, background, and cutout assets

Every generated frame references the same bible and continuity ID. Do not regenerate a new visual style sentence by sentence.

## Semantic sequence

Break the narration into beats that change action, goal, emotion, location, or explanation. One illustration may cover several sentences. Use establishing, action, detail, reaction, and resolution views to maintain spatial logic.

## Limited animation

Create motion by separating layers and applying restrained pan, zoom, parallax, mask reveal, cutout movement, eye blink, light shift, or foreground pass. Four to five intentional poses/frames may sell an action; do not simulate high-frame-rate character animation with inconsistent images.

## Consistency QA

- same character, clothes, important props, colors, and scene geometry
- no unexplained left/right reversal
- stable facial and hand design
- text-free generation unless text is later typeset by the renderer
- no fake product UI or evidence
- no sudden style, lens, or material change
- each motion ends in a readable held composition

If continuity cannot be maintained, fall back to a single well-directed illustration plus camera/layer motion.
